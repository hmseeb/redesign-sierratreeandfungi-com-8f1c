New Homeowner Tree Assessment in El Dorado County

A tree can look healthy and still be one season away from failure. As a new homeowner in El Dorado County, California, you may have just inherited mature trees you've never managed, trees with hidden structural defects, active decay, or fire risk that no home inspector was trained to find. Sierra Tree and Fungi offers independent arborist assessments to give you an honest, complete picture of every tree on your property.

What New Homeowners Don't Know

Trees don't always announce problems. Here are four things new homeowners often miss:

  • Internal decay: fungal infection can hollow a tree from the inside, often with few outward signs until it fails.
  • Root damage: prior construction or soil compaction can kill roots slowly, over years.
  • Species specific risk: each species has its own strengths and weaknesses, and understanding your trees is the basis of good management.
  • Pests and pathogens: El Dorado County has its own tree pests and pathogens, from bark beetles to wood decay fungi, and knowing them is the foundation of preventative care.

On a foothill property in El Dorado County the trees you have just bought are most likely blue oak, interior live oak, valley oak, or California black oak, and on the larger parcels ponderosa pine, gray pine, and incense cedar as well. Each carries a different liability. A mature valley oak leaning over the house is a four or five figure question if it is failing, and a ponderosa that bark beetles have already taken is an urgent one.

Why Independent Matters

Some tree companies offer free on site assessments, but they're better trained on removal than on understanding what a tree's condition is actually telling them. I run no crews and have no financial incentive to recommend removal. My only job is to tell you the truth about your trees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in a new homeowner tree assessment?

A new homeowner tree assessment is a property walk through with a written report: a species inventory with care notes, a hazard assessment of trees near structures, a defensible space evaluation referencing PRC 4291 and CAL FIRE guidance, and a prioritized action plan that separates urgent issues from long term care. The assessment with its written report starts at $500, and the site visit on its own is $250 for up to one hour. Larger or more complex properties take longer, and I quote that before scheduling. It gives you an honest baseline for the trees you just took on.

Do I need defensible space around my new home?

If your property is in a State Responsibility Area, or in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone within a local responsibility area, yes. California law requires 100 feet of defensible space around homes there, or out to the property line if it is closer (Public Resources Code section 4291 in a State Responsibility Area, Government Code section 51182 in a local Very High zone), achieved through spacing, pruning, and removing dead fuel rather than clearing every tree. I include a defensible space review so you know which trees are sound and which are genuine hazards.
